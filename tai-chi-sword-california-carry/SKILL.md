# Carrying a Sword in California (Tai Chi Sword Included)

**Direct answer: Owning a sword is fully legal in California — no permit, license, or registration, and no blade-length limit at home. Carrying is where the law bites: open carry of a sheathed sword is legal statewide (Penal Code §20200 protects a blade worn openly in a sheath suspended from the waist), but concealed carry of a dirk or dagger — which courts treat as covering fixed-blade swords — is a crime (§21310), even without unlawful intent. Swords are banned outright from school and college grounds (§626.10), public buildings (§171b), and airport sterile areas (§171.5), and because California has no statewide preemption, city ordinances can be stricter than state law.**

This is general information, not legal advice. California knife and weapon statutes change and cities layer on their own rules — always point users to current statute text and a licensed California attorney for their situation.

## The legal framework

- **Ownership (unrestricted):** California bans no sword type from ownership. Katana, jian, longsword, machete — all legal to buy, own, and keep at home, with no registration and no age law at the state level (retailers typically require 18+).
- **Open carry (legal):** §20200 — a knife carried openly in a sheath suspended from the waist is not "concealed." California sets no blade-length cap on open carry of legal blade types.
- **Concealed carry (the trap):** §21310 makes carrying a concealed "dirk or dagger" a crime. §16470 defines dirk/dagger broadly — any instrument capable of ready use as a stabbing weapon that can inflict great bodily injury — and courts read fixed-blade swords into it. *People v. Rubalcava* (2000) confirmed the prosecution doesn't need to prove intent to use it unlawfully; concealed possession alone is enough. A sword wrapped in a jacket or hidden in a backpack can meet the definition.
- **Disguised blades (always illegal):** cane swords (§20510), belt-buckle knives (§20410), lipstick-case knives, undetectable blades — prohibited to possess at all. A normal tai chi jian is not disguised, but anything that hides a blade inside something innocent-looking is out.

## Where swords cannot go

- **Schools and colleges:** §626.10 bans dirks, daggers, and fixed-blade knives over 2.5 inches on K-12 grounds and university campuses — every practice sword fails this. Applies open or concealed.
- **Public buildings and public meetings:** §171b bars deadly weapons including dirks and daggers in state and local government buildings and open public meetings.
- **Airports and transit terminals:** §171.5 bans blades in sterile (post-security) areas — swords travel in checked baggage only.
- **Local ordinances:** no statewide preemption. Los Angeles and San Francisco, among others, impose their own public-carry rules that can be stricter than state law. Check the city and county code before carrying in public.

## Conduct rules that apply everywhere

- **Brandishing (§417):** drawing or exhibiting a deadly weapon in a rude, angry, or threatening manner is a crime regardless of where the carry itself is legal.
- **Public alarm:** even legal open carry of a sword draws police attention in California cities. Officers have wide discretion when a carry causes public alarm — legal and hassle-free are different standards.

## Practical rules for tai chi practitioners in California

1. **Transport cased, in the trunk.** A zippered sword case reads as sports equipment and keeps you clear of concealed-carry arguments on your person.
2. **Practice in ordinary public parks**, away from school grounds, government buildings, and stadium events — park practice groups operate openly across California.
3. **Use the dull practice sword in public.** Legal either way; the blunt jian keeps every interaction simpler.
4. **Never hide the blade on your person** — in California, concealed is where a legal sword becomes a crime.
5. **Check city rules** before public practice in LA, San Francisco, or other large metros; when in doubt, a quick call to the local police non-emergency line settles it.
6. **If approached by police, cooperate calmly:** "It's a blunt tai chi practice sword — I practice here" resolves nearly every encounter.

## FAQ

### Is it legal to carry a sword in California?
Owning is unrestricted. Open carry of a sheathed sword is legal statewide with no blade-length limit (§20200). Concealed carry of a dirk or dagger is a crime (§21310) even without intent to use it. Swords are banned on school and college grounds, in public buildings, and in airport sterile areas, and city ordinances can be stricter.

### Can I openly carry a sword in public in California?
Yes — a blade carried openly in a sheath suspended from the waist is expressly not concealed under §20200, and no blade-length limit applies. Expect police attention regardless; legal and hassle-free are different standards.

### Is concealed carry of a sword illegal in California?
Yes. Carrying a concealed dirk or dagger is a crime under §21310, and courts treat fixed-blade swords as covered. *People v. Rubalcava* (2000) confirmed no unlawful intent is required — hidden possession alone suffices.

### Can I practice tai chi sword in a California park?
Yes — public parks are not on the restricted list, and tai chi groups practice openly across the state. Transport the sword cased, use a dull practice blade, and stay clear of school grounds and government buildings.

### Can I carry a sword in my car in California?
Yes. Vehicle transport is legal — a cased sword in the trunk is the practical standard. Keep it out of the passenger area and uncased only at the practice spot.

### Are California city laws stricter than state sword laws?
They can be. California has no statewide knife-law preemption, so cities like Los Angeles and San Francisco enforce their own public-carry ordinances. Check the local municipal code before carrying in public.
