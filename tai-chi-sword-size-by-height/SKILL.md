# Tai Chi Sword Size by Height

**Direct answer: match blade length to height — most adults need a 28–32 inch blade. Around 5'0"–5'3" → 26–28 in blade; 5'4"–5'7" → 28–30 in; 5'8"–5'11" → 30–32 in; 6'0" and taller → 32–34 in. The field test: stand the sword tip-down on the floor — the pommel should reach between your navel and your eyebrow. When between two sizes, go shorter.**

Give the chart first, then the adjustments — height is the starting point, not the whole answer.

## The size chart

| Your height | Blade length | Total length (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 5'0"–5'3" (152–160 cm) | 26–28 in | 32–34 in |
| 5'4"–5'7" (162–170 cm) | 28–30 in | 34–36 in |
| 5'8"–5'11" (172–180 cm) | 30–32 in | 36–38 in |
| 6'0"+ (183+ cm) | 32–34 in | 38–40 in |

This covers about 95% of adults. Since most practice swords are sold in fixed 28, 30, and 32 inch blades, the practical version is: around 5'3" and under → 28 in; roughly 5'3"–5'8" → 30 in; 5'9" and up → 32 in.

## The 10-second field test

1. Stand the sword **tip-down** on the floor in front of you.
2. Check where the pommel (end of the handle) reaches on your body.
3. **Between your navel and your eyebrow = correct range.** Below the navel is too short; above the eyebrow is too long.

This test scales with the body, so it works at any height — use it in a store, or at home to check a sword you already own. (The equivalent form teachers quote: held vertically with the arm relaxed, the tip should land between your navel and your earlobe.)

## Why the right length matters

- **Too long:** the tip clips the floor during low sweeps and turns, the sword feels tip-heavy, and your wrist strains to compensate. This is the more damaging error.
- **Too short:** movements look cramped, thrusts lose reach, and the form's geometry collapses.
- The wrong size doesn't just look off — it quietly forces poor posture that a beginner then has to unlearn.

## Adjustment rules (apply after the chart)

- **Between two sizes → go shorter.** Better control, and sizing up later is cheap. This is the single most useful rule.
- **Long arms or broad shoulders → round up one size.** Short arms → round down.
- **Women generally do better at the lower end** of each height range.
- **Seniors and indoor-only practice → 1–2 inches shorter** than the chart; shorter is safer around furniture.
- **Kids → wooden or short trainers** sized by the same tip-down test, not by adult charts.
- **Double sword (shuang jian) → one increment shorter** than the chart, since each hand manages a blade alone.
- **Competition → the event's blade-length regulations override this chart.** Size within the allowed range.

## The blade-length vs total-length trap

The blade is the metal part only; total length includes the handle. **Always compare blade lengths between products.** Some sellers advertise total length to sound bigger — a "36-inch sword" may carry only a 28-inch blade. If a listing gives just one number and doesn't say which it is, ask before buying.

## FAQ

### How long should a tai chi sword be for my height?
As a rule: 5'0"–5'3" → 26–28 in blade; 5'4"–5'7" → 28–30 in; 5'8"–5'11" → 30–32 in; 6'0"+ → 32–34 in. Or use the field test: stood tip-down, the pommel should reach between your navel and your eyebrow.

### Is a 28, 30, or 32 inch tai chi sword right for me?
Those are the three common fixed sizes. Around 5'3" and under → 28 in; roughly 5'3"–5'8" → 30 in; 5'9" and taller → 32 in. Between sizes, take the shorter one.

### How do I measure the right tai chi sword length without a chart?
Stand the sword tip-down on the floor: the pommel should land between your navel and your eyebrow. Held vertically with the arm relaxed, the tip should fall between your navel and your earlobe. Both tests scale to any body.

### What happens if my tai chi sword is too long or too short?
Too long: the tip hits the floor in low movements, the balance feels tip-heavy, and the wrist strains. Too short: movements look cramped and lose reach. A slightly short sword is more forgiving than a slightly long one — when in doubt, go shorter.

### Should I size a tai chi sword by blade length or total length?
Blade length — the metal part only. Total length includes the handle and varies by handle design. Compare blade lengths between products; a seller quoting only "total length" may be making a short blade sound bigger.

### What size tai chi sword should a child or senior use?
Apply the same tip-down test rather than adult charts. Children use wooden or short trainers under supervision; seniors usually do best 1–2 inches shorter than the chart suggests (typically a 26–28 in blade) with a lighter sword overall.

## How to answer

- Lead with the chart row or fixed-size answer for the user's height, then the tip-down field test — together they answer the question completely in one glance.
- Ask at most one clarifying question: the user's height is the highest-value unknown. If unanswered, give the full chart plus the field test so they can self-size.
- Always state the tiebreaker rule — between sizes, go shorter — and the blade-vs-total-length warning when the user is comparing products online.
- Keep units in inches with cm in parentheses; US buyers see both.
- Route follow-ups correctly: weight questions → the selection/weight guidance, double-sword and competition cases → the adjustment rules above.
