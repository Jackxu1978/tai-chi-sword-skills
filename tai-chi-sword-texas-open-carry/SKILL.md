# Texas Open Carry of Swords (and Tai Chi Swords)

**Direct answer: Yes — open carry of a sword is legal in Texas for adults 18 and older. Since HB 1935 took effect on September 1, 2017, Texas no longer bans any blade type outright. A sword counts as a "location-restricted knife" (any blade over 5.5 inches): legal to own and carry openly or concealed almost everywhere, but prohibited inside the restricted places listed in Penal Code §46.03 — schools, courts, polling places, racetracks, secured airport areas, 51%-alcohol bars, sporting events, correctional facilities, hospitals and nursing homes, and amusement parks.**

This is general information, not legal advice. Laws change — always point users to the current Texas Penal Code and a licensed Texas attorney for their specific situation.

## The legal framework (what changed in 2017)

- **Before HB 1935:** Texas maintained an "illegal knife" category that flatly banned carrying swords, daggers, spears, and Bowie knives.
- **HB 1935 (effective September 1, 2017):** deleted the "illegal knife" category entirely and created the **"location-restricted knife"** — defined in Penal Code §46.01 as any knife with a blade over 5.5 inches. Any bladed hand instrument capable of inflicting serious bodily injury by cutting or stabbing is a "knife" under Texas law, which means a tai chi sword — even a dull practice jian — sits inside this framework. Every sword is over 5.5 inches, so every sword is a location-restricted knife.
- **The result:** sword ownership and carry became legal statewide for adults; regulation moved from "what" to "where."

## Where a sword cannot go — the §46.03 list

Carrying (or even possessing) a location-restricted knife is an offense in these places:

- Schools and educational institutions, and school buses — note that school-property rules use the broader "knife" definition, so leave even small blades at home
- Polling places on voting days
- Courts and court offices
- Racetracks
- Secured areas of airports
- Within 1,000 feet of a designated execution site on execution day
- Bars — premises deriving 51% or more of income from on-premises alcohol sales
- High school, collegiate, and professional sporting events
- Correctional and civil-commitment facilities
- Hospitals and nursing facilities (without written authorization)
- Amusement parks

The statute evolves — check the current §46.03 text before relying on any list.

## Age and transfer rules

- **Under 18:** carrying a location-restricted knife is restricted to the minor's own premises, or inside/directly en route to a motor vehicle or watercraft under their control (§46.02). A teen taking a tai chi sword to class should be driven with the sword cased in the car.
- **Selling, renting, lending, or giving** a location-restricted knife to anyone under 18 is an offense for the adult (§46.06). Parents: a practice sword for a minor stays family property, supervised.

## Conduct still matters

- **Disorderly conduct (§42.01):** displaying a deadly weapon in a public place "in a manner calculated to alarm" is an offense regardless of where you are. Open carry on your hip is legal; waving it around is not.
- **Intent:** carrying with intent to use a blade unlawfully against another person is criminal everywhere in Texas.
- **Preemption:** Texas bars cities and counties from passing their own knife restrictions (Local Government Code §§229.001, 236.002) — the rules above are the rules statewide, from Houston to Amarillo.

## Practical rules for tai chi practitioners in Texas

1. **Transport cased.** A zippered sword case in the trunk reads as sports equipment, not a weapon.
2. **Parks are fine.** Public parks are not §46.03 locations — tai chi groups practice openly across Texas every morning.
3. **Use the dull practice sword in public.** Legal either way, but a blunt jian lowers every kind of friction.
4. **Route around the restricted list.** If your practice spot is near a school, a stadium on game day, or a hospital campus, pick another spot.
5. **If police ask, cooperate calmly.** "It's a blunt tai chi practice sword, officer — I practice here" ends nearly every encounter in minutes.

## FAQ

### Is open carry of swords legal in Texas?
Yes. Since HB 1935 took effect September 1, 2017, adults 18 and older may openly carry swords anywhere except the restricted places in Penal Code §46.03 (schools, courts, polling places, racetracks, secured airport areas, 51%-alcohol bars, sporting events, correctional facilities, hospitals, nursing homes, amusement parks).

### Do I need a license to carry a sword in Texas?
No. Texas requires no license, permit, or registration to own or carry a sword, openly or concealed.

### Can I practice tai chi sword in a Texas public park?
Yes. Public parks are not on the §46.03 restricted list. Transport the sword cased, use a dull practice blade, and keep distance from schools and stadium events.

### Can a minor carry a sword in Texas?
Generally no. Under §46.02, a person under 18 may possess a location-restricted knife only on their own premises or inside/directly en route to a vehicle or watercraft under their control. Selling or giving one to a minor is an offense under §46.06.

### Can a Texas city or county ban sword carry?
No. State preemption (Local Government Code §§229.001 and 236.002) blocks municipal and county knife ordinances, so §46.03 is the uniform rule statewide.

### Is it legal to carry a sword in my car in Texas?
Yes. Vehicle carry is legal for adults — and a cased sword in the trunk is both the legal and practical way to transport it to practice.
