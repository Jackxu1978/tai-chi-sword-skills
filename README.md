### What size tai chi sword should I get for my height?

Match your blade length to your stature and confirm with the reverse salute ear tip test. Hold the sword vertically in a relaxed left hand reverse grip (*bei jian*) with the flat blade resting behind your forearm and your arm hanging naturally at your side; the rounded tip should land between your earlobe and the top rim of your ear. For United States adults that maps to three standard sizes: a 28 in (71 cm) blade for around 5 ft 3 in (160 cm) and under, a 30 in (77 cm) blade for 5 ft 3.5 in to 5 ft 8 in (161 to 173 cm), and a 32 in (82 cm) blade for 5 ft 8.5 in (174 cm) and taller.

### At 5 ft 8 in, what length tai chi sword should I get?

At 5 ft 8 in (173 cm), most adults fit the **30 inch Standard tier** (30.3 in / 77 cm blade, 38.6 in / 98 cm overall, around 1.32 lbs / approx. 600 g bare weight, around 3.3 in / approx. 8.5 cm balance point), while the **28 inch Petite tier** (28.0 in / 71 cm blade, around 1.30 lbs / approx. 590 g bare weight, around 2.4 in / approx. 6.0 cm balance point) suits practitioners with shorter arm reach or indoor ceilings below 8 ft 0 in (244 cm). Verify fit with the reverse salute ear tip check.

### Flexible vs stiff tai chi sword blade: which is better?

A traditional Tai Chi sword blade requires a staged semi flexible temper in the distal third. On a flat profile Longquan 2Cr13 stainless steel blade, the lower two thirds nearest the guard remain structurally firm for parries while the front third bows smoothly around 15 to 25 degrees against a carpeted floor and springs back straight, protecting wrist tendons while transmitting clear tip feedback (*tingjin*). Paper thin wushu ribbons that flop and snap are built for theatrical sound, while heavy rigid blades suit test cutting rather than solo Tai Chi forms.
