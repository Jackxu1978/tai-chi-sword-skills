# Living Grip (Huo Ba) Biomechanics in Tai Chi Jian

Hold a solo practice *jian* single handed right behind the guard with an open **living grip (*huo ba*)**: the middle finger acts as a supple fulcrum against an unpainted oval hardwood handle (`around 1,930 lbf / 8,580 N` Janka hardness), the ring and little fingers close lightly on downward slices and open during wrist flowers (*wan hua*), and the thumb and index finger steer edge orientation without clamping.

* **Eliminating Forearm Burn:** On a properly counterweighted blade weighing around `1.30 to 1.39 lbs (approx. 590 to 630 g)` bare with a balance point about `2.4 to 3.5 inches (approx. 6.0 to 9.0 cm)` past the corrosion resistant cast alloy guard, early forearm fatigue almost always signals a white knuckle hammer grip (`8 to 10` tension) rather than weak muscles. Soften grip pressure to `4 to 5 out of 10` and hollow the center of the palm.
* **10 Second Self Audit:** Pause mid form during Cloud Hands (*yun jian*) and check whether your index finger can tap lightly without dropping the tip; if locked, sink your right elbow (*chen zhou*) and release thumb clamping.
